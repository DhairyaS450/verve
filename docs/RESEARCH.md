# Research: what the best coaches actually drill

Verve's content is built from the practice methods of working coaches, not from theory. This file records the sources and how each one became a skill, a drill, or a benchmark inside the app.

## Vinh Giang (Stage Academy, masterclass, free resources)

- **Five vocal foundations**: rate, volume, pitch/melody, tonality, pause. Volume target 6–7 of 10. Pause is authority.
  → Skills: `rate`, `volume`, `pitch`, `tonality`, `pause`. Warmups: `w-volume-dial`, `w-rate-contrast`, `w-siren`, `w-pause-punctuation`, `w-four-emotions`.
- **Siren technique** for pitch range. → `w-siren`, `w-high-low`.
- **3-2-1**: three steps, two types, one thing. Anti-rambling for random topics. → `three-two-one`, `m-321`, `w-three-things`.
- **PREP** (Point, Reason, Example, Point) and **PARA** (Point, Action, Result, Ask). → `prep`, `para`, `m-prep-answer`, `m-para-update`.
- **Story formula**: incident (who/what/where/when) → relive it → "the reason I'm telling you this is…". 2–3 minutes max. VAKS sensory detail. Reporting is dull, reliving is magnetic. → `story-formula`, `relive`, `vaks`, `m-story-formula`, `m-relive`, `m-vaks`.
- **Four vocal archetypes**: Friend, Motivator, Educator, Coach; sequence Friend → Motivator → Educator → Coach → Friend. → `vocal-archetypes`, `w-archetype-read`, `m-keynote-10`.
- **AMS**: analogy, metaphor, simile. → `analogies`, `w-analogy-sprint`, `m-analogy-explain`.
- **Body**: string-from-the-crown posture, "power sphere" gestures (belly to eyes), exaggerated facial expressions, lens eye contact. → `posture`, `gestures`, `facial-expression`, `eye-contact`.
- **Nerves**: burn adrenaline before speaking. → `nerves`, `w-adrenaline`.
- **Self-review**: review audio only for tone, video without sound for body, transcript for fillers. → Verve's review page splits audio metrics, video, and transcript with fillers highlighted.
- **Four stages of mastery / Shu-Ha-Ri**: practice for months, one layer at a time. → progressive-overload planner: one fix per session.

## Matt Abrahams (Think Faster, Talk Smarter; Stanford GSB)

- Structures: **What / So What / Now What**, **Problem-Solution-Benefit**, **ADD** (Answer, Detail, Describe value), STAR, 1-3-1 talk. → `what-so-what`, `problem-solution-benefit`, `add`, `one-three-one`, `talk-131`.
- Spontaneity drills: paraphrasing and summarizing, "athletes drill so they can respond in the game". → `paraphrase`, `m-paraphrase`, the whole `spontaneity` branch.
- Filler trick: end the thought on the exhale and inhale immediately; you cannot say "um" while inhaling. → `w-one-breath`, `fillers` cue.
- Grounding: count backwards by sevens, tongue twisters. → `w-count-back`.

## Yasir Khan (Speak Like a CEO, 10-day bootcamp)

- Articulation: vowel sounds, open the mouth wider. → `vowels`, `w-vowel-stretch`, `articulators`.
- **Contrast structure** for questions with many answers: "It's not about X, it's about Y." → `contrast`, `m-contrast`.
- 10 days of daily practice produce visible change. → Verve's daily plan and visible metrics.

## Jun Yuh (storytelling for attention)

- Capture attention in the first seconds; is there a transformation; what is the payoff. → `hook`, `m-hook-five`, `landing`, `stakes`.

## Simon Sinek, Nancy Duarte, Randy Olson, Kenn Adams, Alan Monroe

- **Golden Circle** (Why → How → What). → `golden-circle`, `m-golden-circle`.
- **Sparkline** (what is / what could be / new bliss). → `sparkline`, `m-sparkline`.
- **ABT** (And, But, Therefore). → `abt`, `m-stakes`.
- **Story spine** (once upon a time… until one day… because of that…). → `story-spine`, `m-story-spine`.
- **Motivated sequence** (attention, need, satisfaction, visualization, action). → `monroe`, `m-monroe`.

## Improv and actor training

- Yes-and, word association, one-word story, "expert interview" → `yes-and`, `word-association`, `expert-interview`, `w-yes-and`, `w-word-association`, `m-expert-interview`.
- Lip trills, sirens, tongue twisters, "me-may-ma-moh-moo", jaw massage, pen-in-mouth over-articulation → `w-lip-trill`, `w-twister-ladder`, `w-face-gym`, `w-pen-read`.

## Benchmarks used in Vero's scoring

- Fillers: about 1 per minute is elite (Quantified Communications); 5 per minute is average (Noah Zandan); 12 per minute measurably lowers ratings.
- Pace: 120–150 wpm for presentations (National Communication Association); 140–160 conversational; above 160 reduces comprehension of complex material; below 100–110 disengages.
- Pauses: deliberate 1–2 second pauses at sentence ends read as control, not weakness.

## Roleplay prep (DECA / FBLA)

Everything in the Roleplay track is transcribed from official documents; nothing is invented.

- **DECA formats** (DECA Guide 2024-25, Individual Series overview; DECA Direct "An Introduction to DECA Role-plays"): Principles events — 1 participant, 10 min prep, 10 min with the judge, 4 performance indicators. Individual Series — 10 / 10, 5 indicators. Team Decision Making — 2 participants, 30 min prep, 15 min presentation. Personal Financial Literacy — 10 / 10, 3 indicators. After the solution, the judge asks the scenario's follow-up questions, then thanks the participant. Notes made in prep may be used; no printed materials or props.
- **DECA rubrics** (Judge's Evaluation Forms on the 2025 and 2026 district sample events): Individual Series — 5 indicators × 14 (Little/No Value 0–4, Below 5–8, Meets 9–11, Exceeds 12–14) + five 21st Century Skills × 6 (0–1 / 2–3 / 4 / 5–6) = 100. 2026 Principles — 4 indicators × 12 (Novice 0–3, Developing 4–7, Proficient 8–11, Exemplary 12) + Solution (unique, practical, effective) 3 × 8 + Career competencies (critical thinking, communication, decision making) 3 × 6 + overall career readiness 10 = 100. 2026 Team Decision Making — 5 × 10 + 3 × 8 + 3 × 6 + overall 8 = 100. PFL — 3 × 24 + 4 × 7 = 100. Band definitions: Exceeds = top 10% of business personnel; Meets = 70–89th percentile; Below = 50–69th; Little/No Value = 0–49th.
- **FBLA formats** (2025-2026 Competitive Events Guidelines, Customer Service and Sports & Entertainment Management, public copies hosted by Virginia CTE Resource Center): 20 min prep with a one-minute warning, 7 min presentation with a one-minute warning, no separate Q&A, judges may ask questions throughout, two notecards per competitor written on both sides, no technology or props. Role play round scores alone decide winners.
- **FBLA rating sheets**: Customer Service (individual) — understanding/defines problem 10, communicates position 10, logical solution and implementation 10, empathy/diplomacy 20, conflict resolution and closure 20, organized statements 10, confidence/body language/eye contact/voice 10, answers questions 10 = 100. Team-style events — understanding 10, alternatives with pros and cons 20, solution and implementation 20, knowledge areas 20, organized 10, confidence 10, answers questions 10 = 100. Bands: Not Demonstrated 0, Below 1–6 (or 1–9), Meets 7–8 (10–16), Exceeds 9–10 (17–20).
- **Cases**: 90 DECA district sample events (2024–2026, every Principles, Individual Series, Team Decision Making and PFL event) and 12 FBLA sample cases (Competitive Events Study Guide 2017–20; TeachFBLA 2019 samples). Situations are condensed by `scripts/build-cases.mjs` with strict fact preservation; performance indicators and judge questions are verbatim; every case links to its source PDF. The pasted-case option lets a competitor practise on any official PDF they hold.
- **Judge advice used in Vero's prompt and the run-of-show** (DECA Direct "In the Judge's Mind"; volunteer-judge articles): the first seconds decide the mood (handshake, eye contact, posture); most points come from the indicators; include who/what/when/how and costs, resources, timelines; tell a cohesive story (restate the issue, identify the gap, explain the solution); stay in character after mistakes; thank the judge.
